Has the Bitter Lesson finally come for SLAM?

Probabilistic state estimation in the era of foundation models. Two motions, two structured debates, and a room that votes before and after.

See the motions Submit a paper Program
Why this workshop

The front end lost. Is the back end next?

Deep learning has brought Sutton's Bitter Lesson to many fields, but for SLAM the verdict is still split. Hand-crafted features, tuned RANSAC and bag-of-words loop closure have given way to learned alternatives. The probabilistic back end has not, and it can claim to be exactly the kind of general method that scales with compute.

Geometric foundation models are now default front ends, semantic embeddings populate maps, and vision-language-action policies bypass state estimation entirely. There is broad consensus that learning and probabilistic inference should be combined, but end-to-end models ask whether that combination is needed at all.

The core question
If a learned model can output the state directly, why should its output still enter a probabilistic inference problem?

End-to-end learning expects the remaining errors to vanish with data and scale. Estimation theory provides a characterized uncertainty and the means to detect failures, which a single network lacks, while foundation models produce strong, structured and correlated errors, particularly out of distribution.

Our position: probabilistic estimation becomes more necessary, not less. This position is falsifiable, so we put it to a vote.

The debates

Two motions. Two sides. One vote.

Leading researchers argue for and against each motion. The audience, in the room and online, votes before and after. The shift is the result.

Moderated by Michael Milford · QUT
M1
Motion 1 · Scale vs. principle

This house believes that out-of-distribution failures in robot state estimation will be solved by more data and scale before they are solved by estimation theory.

14:00–15:00
Proposition

Data and scale will close the out-of-distribution gap first, just as they did for the front end.

Call for papers

Bring evidence. Take a seat on stage.

We invite short papers of up to four pages providing evidence for or against either motion. Position papers, negative results and failure-case analyses are explicitly welcome. Submissions are non-archival and reviewed double-blind by at least two reviewers. Authors may indicate which motion their work supports.

Topics include, but are not limited to

  • Integrating foundation-model outputs (poses, depth, correspondences) into SLAM
  • Characterizing uncertainty, calibration and error correlation of learned perception for state estimation
  • Semantic and language-embedded maps for robot perception
  • Implicit, end-to-end learned state versus explicit state estimation for navigation and control
  • Introspection and out-of-distribution failure detection
  • Consistency, observability and safety guarantees of estimators with learned inputs
  • Benchmarks and datasets comparing learned and probabilistic approaches to SLAM
